What this is. A feed on schooling worldwide, in twenty-four subjects: the factory model it was built on and the workforce it was built to supply, curriculum homogenisation and the local and Indigenous knowledge it displaces, the textbook oligopoly and its mergers, private equity and the privatisation of public schooling, for-profit colleges and diploma mills, student debt and credential inflation, the elite university pipeline into political office, the capture of global education governance, edtech contracts and vendor lock-in, knowledge moved behind a paywall, student data as a corporate asset, ecosystem conditioning from an early age, teachers replaced by technology, the testing industry, teachers' own conditions, what may and may not be taught, what endowments invest in, who is kept out, and the learning being built outside all of it. Nothing here is written or summarised: headlines and snippets are the publishers' own, every row links to the original, and no model touches the text.

What it is not. School life as an event listing is not a subject. A sports result, a prize day, a school play, a term-dates notice are refused. So is the marketing that surrounds education — sponsored rankings, course discounts, study-abroad offers. What this wire keeps is schooling as a system: who owns it, who writes what is taught, what it costs, what it does with the people inside it, and what it is for. Every subject term is gated behind a schooling or accountability word so the feed cannot fill with newsletter copy.

Standing. Every wire is labelled by what kind of source it is, and every row carries the label. Ministries, inspectorates & UN bodies is the bodies that set, fund or inspect education — education ministries, school inspectorates, courts, the OECD, the World Bank, UNESCO. Research & education data is the institutes and statistical work. Unions & campaigning groups is the organisations arguing with all of it — teachers' unions, and the privacy and corporate-accountability groups that follow the contracts and the student data. Press is general news across 26 languages. Standing is provenance, not a claim that a story is important.

Weight. Separately, each story is scored on what it carries: a decision — a ruling, a law enacted, a contract awarded, a merger cleared, an accreditation stripped (2); institutional material — a ministry or inspectorate report, official statistics, a UNESCO or OECD publication, a peer-reviewed study (2); a measured figure such as a per-pupil sum, a market share or a contract value (1); a pending decision with a date attached (1); a named jurisdiction (1); and a primary source (1). At three or more the row is marked consequential, and the Weight filter narrows to those. A campaign statement and a national curriculum ruling both appear here; the pips are how you tell them apart at a glance, and the words beside them say what earned the score.

What is refused. School life as event listing — fixtures, prom, prize day, term dates — along with fiction and entertainment that uses the vocabulary, and sponsored ranking and discount copy. Campus policing and shootings belong to the law enforcement wire and are refused here. The status line reports how many stories each harvest refused.

Translation. The Translate control at the top renders the headlines and snippets on screen into any of 26 languages, through a free public translation endpoint — no account, no key, nothing stored. A translated row is marked as machine translation and keeps the publisher's original wording on hover, because a machine rendering is not the publisher's words. If the endpoint is unavailable the row simply stays in its original language rather than emptying. Translation applies to what is on screen; scroll further or widen a filter and press the control again.

Region. Placement runs three levels deep. Pick a region and a second row opens with its subregions; pick one of those and a third row lists the places inside it — Latin America to the Amazon Basin to the Brazilian Amazon, Oceania to Australia to the Northern Territory. Each row counts what it holds, so an empty subregion is visible rather than hidden, and every story files under the subregion and region above whatever place it names.

Limits worth knowing. The gate is mechanical: it reads words, not meaning. Standing is assigned per wire rather than per article. Google News caps a query at roughly 100 results over about 30 days. Coverage is uneven by language and the counts show it rather than hiding it.

Every wire read on the last harvest, with its standing: what it returned, and how many of its stories were refused. A wire that could not be reached says so rather than disappearing.
